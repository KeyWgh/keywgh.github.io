“Expectation for lab"

Aug 8, 2020

The motivation to write this article is that I was notified my Spring 2020 Student Rating of Teaching (SRT) results were available recently. After one year’s teaching for STAT 3011 and 3021, and talks with some professors, I think it’s beneficial to rethink what should I do to help and support my students. Also it’s a try to let you know my thoughts and style as a TA.

For undergrad courses

The first year I only taught two undergrad level classes, hence many students were not familiar with statistics or even essential mathematics, while the load of course was heavy.

Speed of lab

From the comments of students enrolled in my sections, there are two opposite voices: one is that they didn’t learn anything for lab materials are similar to lecture, and I was too slow; another is they felt difficult to catch up, and I should show more practice problems and explanations. Usually I would ask my students how do they feel about the speed so I have a better control of the flow (which is inspired from Prof. Muary Bramson). Although I often forget to ask, it do works well. And from the responses, the majority of students attend undergrad level courses prefer to just keep with materials.

This also agrees with DGS’s opinion. We think the lab is mainly to help students who have difficulties to understand the lecture, so materials also focus on additional/supplementary exercise. We’re doing our best to guarantee that most student learn the basic ideas and tools of statistics, hence in the future, they may use statistical knowledge to address their concerns in general fields. As for brilliant students, we believe you have the ability to explore extra contents by yourself. While as a TA, I’m willing to answer your questions such as where to go and what is important and so on.

Materials of lab

This is not decided by TA, we have little to do with the content of materials. Some students may prefer theory while others prefer applications, like how to use R, but you have to accept the materials. It’s just a part of the course, no matter you like it or not.

What I can do for you is that, if possible, I’d love to show my understanding of the relationship among abstract terms, and connection between theory and applications. Not every one can understand so-called “high-level” perspectives, for sometimes those interpretations requires background knowledge not covered in current class. If I say too much of this, students lack necessary background will feel confused and lose their interests.

Interactions with students

I have to say it’s the most difficult part, especially for theory. I had several talks with other professors about this, and it seems hard for every teacher to effectively interact with students. It’s pretty common each time when I ask a question, the students willing to response are always the same group(1-3 persons typically), which is quite embarrassing.

It would be great if you can share your suggestions with me.

For graduate course

I haven’t taught any graduate class yet, so I leave this part blank for further revision. I expect I would talk more theoretical stuff.

Conclusions

Our main purpose is to help the majority of you better understand the lecture. Hence the speed and difficulty level varies. I think the vote is the fairest way to decide how the form of lab for each class. As a result, please feel free to tell me your true thought, via voting or email or any other approach. Do not keep your concern until the end of semester and leave it on SRT, or worse, say nothing but think this lab is totally useless.

Thanks for reading.
