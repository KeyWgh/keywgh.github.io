The motivation to write this article is that I have been notified my Spring 2020 Student Rating of Teaching (SRT) results were available recently. After one year’s teaching for STAT 3011 and 3021, and talks with some professors, I think it’s beneficial to rethink that what should I do to help and support my students. Also, it’s a try to let you know my thoughts and style as a TA.

## For undergrad courses

The first year I only taught two undergrad level classes, where many students were not familiar with statistics or even essential mathematics, while the course load was heavy. 

### Speed of lab

From the comments of students enrolled in my sections, there are two opposite viewpoints: one is that they didn’t learn anything for lab materials are similar to lecture, and I was too slow; another is they felt struggling to catch up, and I should show more practice problems and explanations. Usually, I would ask my students how do they feel about the speed, so I have better control of the flow (which is inspired by Prof. Maury Bramson). Although I often forget to ask, it does work well. And from the responses, the majority of students attending undergrad level courses prefer to keep with materials. 

I once consulted with DGS about this. In his opinion, the lab is mainly to help students who have difficulties in understanding the lecture. Hence, materials also focus on additional/supplementary exercise. I agree with this point. We’re doing our best to guarantee that most students learn the basic ideas and tools of statistics. In the future, they may use statistical knowledge to address their concerns in general fields. As for brilliant students, we believe you have the ability to explore extra content by yourself. As a TA, I’m willing to answer your questions like where to go and what is important. 

### Materials of lab

This is not decided by the TA. Some students may prefer theory while others prefer applications, such as using R, but you have to accept the materials. It’s just a part of the course, no matter you like it or not.

If possible, I’d love to show my understanding of the relationship between abstract terms and connections between theory and applications. Not everyone can understand so-called “high-level” perspectives, for sometimes those interpretations require background knowledge not covered in the current class. If I say too much of this, students lack the necessary background will feel confused and lose their interests. 

### Interactions with students

I have to say it’s the most challenging part, especially for theory. I had several talks with other professors about this. It seems hard for every teacher to interact with students effectively. It’s pretty common each time when I ask a question, the students willing to respond are always the same group(1-3 persons typically), which is quite embarrassing. 

It would be great if you can share your suggestions with me. 

## For graduate courses

I haven’t taught any graduate class yet, so I leave this part blank for further revision. I expect I would talk more theoretical stuff. 

## Conclusions

Our primary purpose is to help the majority of you better understand the lecture. Hence the speed and difficulty level varies. I think the vote is the fairest way to decide how the lab form for each class. As a result, please feel free to tell me your real thoughts via voting or email or any other approach. Do not keep your concern until the end of the semester and leave it on SRT, or worse, say nothing but think this lab is useless. 

Thanks for reading.
